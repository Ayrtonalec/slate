# God powers & the world

## World creation

Two doors in, same engine underneath:

1. **Blank slate:** fantasy-map-maker workflow — brush-paint elevation, rivers, biomes, and coastlines with instant cartographic feedback (think Azgaar/Inkarnate fluency, but the map then *comes alive*). This mode alone overlaps a proven hobby audience.
2. **Presets & seeds:** curated worlds (Earth-like, archipelago, pangaea, brutal north, shattered isles), plus procedural seeds with share codes. Preset worlds are also our tutorial surface — a preset with a starving coastal culture teaches weather powers better than any tooltip.

*"Real-world 2026" presets are an expansion, not base game — see the tech dial below.*

## Power taxonomy

Seven verbs, so the kit stays legible. Every power emits chronicle events + Interpretation tags (doc 03) — no exceptions, or the faith system develops blind spots.

| Verb | Examples | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Shape** | Raise/carve terrain, rivers, climate zones | The map-builder verbs, usable mid-game too (earthquakes are just angry Shape) |
| **Seed** | Minerals, forests, species, monsters | The gold-cascade verb. Seeding a *creature* seeds an ecology, not a unit |
| **Touch** | Bless/heal/smite an individual or field | The prayer-answering verbs; small, cheap, intimate |
| **Sign** | Visions, omens, dreams, weather portents | The *communication channel* — how subtle gods play. Cryptic by design: mortals interpret signs through their existing doctrine, sometimes disastrously |
| **Wrath** | Storms, plagues, quakes, floods | Discounted in Dread. Aftermath is content: ruins, refugees, relics, pilgrimage sites |
| **Wonder** | Persistent miracles: eternal spring, floating isle, golden mountain | Devotion-hungry upkeep (doc 03); the prestige verbs |
| **Avatar** | Walk the world at ground level | Big faith interactions, festivals, judgment scenes; the ground-zoom's headline activity along with prayers |

Design rule: powers don't have cooldowns — they have *consequences*. Pacing comes from the faith economy and from watching cascades play out, which suits the chill positioning.

## The tech-pace dial (resolving "fantasy vs. 2026 NYC")

The founder notes want both medieval fantasy *and* dragons-over-modern-Manhattan. Those are wildly different asset/sim universes — but there's an elegant reframe:

**Technological progress speed is a divine lever.** Bless inquiry and industry, and eras advance; enshrine tradition (or smite the printing press as heresy), and the world plateaus in a medieval stasis — *diegetically explaining* why D&D-style worlds stay medieval for millennia. Player choice, expressed through play, not a settings menu.

- **Base game:** Year 0 → late-medieval cap. Bounds art scope hard (one architectural/costume universe), matches Manor Lords aesthetics, serves the core audience precisely. Fantasy creatures included from day 1 — a dragon over a medieval city is base game.
- **"Modern Age" expansion:** uncaps the dial through industrial → modern, adds the 2026 city presets (guns, mass media, states — mechanically: media = instant global rumor propagation, which the myth system already models as a limit case). The dragon-in-NYC trailer moment lives here, where its enormous asset cost is funded by an expansion, not carried by v1.

## Zoom bands (the Manor Lords camera, made honest)

Four bands, each mapped to a sim layer (doc 02), with crafted transitions rather than one lying "seamless" zoom:

1. **Atlas** — the whole world rendered as a *living illustrated map*: parchment/ink cartography (or satellite style, player's choice) where borders creep, cities sprout, routes thicken, and names update as history unfolds. For the map-nerd audience this is the poster feature — your fantasy map that draws itself. Also the time-scrubbing view.
2. **Province** — Total-War-style terrain overview; armies, caravans, weather systems visible as pieces-on-the-land.
3. **Settlement** — Manor Lords altitude; streets, rooftops, crowds; the "beauty shot" band and where the art budget aims.
4. **Ground** — eye level among the theater crowd; prayers audible; Avatar walks here. Intentionally vignette-like (depth-of-field, atmosphere) — intimate framing, which *also* keeps the close-up asset bar survivable (doc 06).

Transition craft (cloud pass-throughs Atlas↔Province, camera swoops below) is a solvable, high-payoff polish problem — it's the trailer's first ten seconds.

## The dynamic-world checklist (the "feels alive" contract)

A standing acceptance list — every system should touch several of these:

- Interventions cascade across ≥2 systems (ecology→economy→culture→faith).
- The world *remembers*: ruins persist, disasters leave scars and relics, droughts leave ghost towns, place names encode events.
- The world *interprets*: every act lands in the Interpretation Engine; myths drift from facts.
- The world *anticipates*: cultures develop rituals aimed at influencing you (sacrifices before storms if you've been wrathful in storm season — they're modeling *you*).
- Nothing is a dead prop: forests regrow, rivers flood and shift, monsters have ecologies, wealth attracts predators (dragons and warlords alike).
