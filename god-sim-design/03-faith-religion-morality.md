# Faith, religion & morality

The founder instinct — "prayer as currency, but NOT a simple one-toned streamlined thing" — is correct, and this doc is the brainstorm it asked for. This system is the game's soul; it's what elevates the sandbox above WorldBox.

## Three currencies, one anti-currency

Faith isn't one number. Mortals generate three distinct resources, and your powers cost *mixes* of them:

| Currency | Generated by | Behavior | Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Awe** | Spectacle — any big visible miracle, good *or* bad | Spikes hard, decays fast | The adrenaline of the divine |
| **Devotion** | Answered prayers, consistency, generations of care | Accrues slowly, compounds, very durable | Trust |
| **Dread** | Punishment, disasters attributed to you | Cheap and fast, but corrodes society (oppression, exodus, zealotry side-effects) | Terror |

- Wrath powers get discounts when paid in Dread; subtle mercies are cheap in Devotion; Wonders (persistent miracles) demand deep Devotion reserves.
- **Playstyles emerge from the economy, not from an alignment slider.** A fear-god runs hot and fast on a brittle society; a love-god compounds slowly into something unshakeable. Most players will be something messier in between — which is the point.
- **The moral hazard is a feature:** disasters spike prayer volume. Crisis literally profits a god. Do you manufacture suffering to farm Awe, or build Devotion the slow way? The game never comments — the world does (see tone, below).

**Doubt** is the anti-currency: a per-culture pool fed by ignored prayers, inconsistency (blessing a village then smiting it), unexplained tragedy, and long divine silence. Doubt is the soil rival religions grow in.

## The Interpretation Engine (the big idea)

**You never choose what kind of god you are. The world reads you.**

Every divine act carries tags (domain: storm/harvest/war/death/sea/craft…; tenor: merciful/wrathful/cryptic…). Each culture runs a running classification of you from *its local evidence* — and builds your religion out of its conclusion:

- Temples, iconography, idols, and vestments generated to match ("the Reaper of the Coast" gets black-sailed shrine-boats; "the Kind Rainbringer" gets cistern-temples).
- Rituals and holidays spawn from specific chronicle events — the anniversary of the day you drowned the invaders becomes a festival *or* a day of dread depending on who's remembering.
- Different cultures can worship the *same* god (you) as *different* gods without knowing it — and can fight wars over interpretations of you. Their theological confusion is chronicled.
- Prophets periodically "canonize" recent interpretation into doctrine. Doctrine lags your behavior — act against canon and you generate Doubt *and* drama (see schisms).

This is the "world feels alive / they react to who you actually are" requirement made mechanical. It's also endlessly streamable: the game keeps showing you *what they think of you*, and it keeps being a funhouse mirror.

## Rival religions — three distinct threats (no combat required)

1. **Schisms (heresies of you).** Inconsistent behavior splits your faith: after you smite a beloved village, a sect insists "the True God would never — that was a false god's deed." Two churches now worship different interpretations of *you*, and may persecute each other. You can feed either, or neither.
2. **Foreign gods.** Born from Doubt around a charismatic prophet Figure. In solo play the rival god is *faceless* — it never acts, it's a faith-drain system wearing a mask, which is both cheap to build and genuinely eerie (you only ever see its works: its temples rising, your pews emptying). In multiplayer, the rival god is *another player* (doc 05).
3. **Secularism (late game).** Enlightenment-adjacent drift reduces the total faith supply as eras advance. The endgame question for a no-opponent game: fight it with wonders and terror, quietly tend the last true believers, or gracefully let yourself become myth — a "retirement ending" where the chronicle's final entry is a folktale about you. (Poignant, on-brand, and turns "the sandbox eventually winds down" from a flaw into an ending.)

## Prayer as moment-to-moment gameplay

God games historically die when zooming in is pointless. Our fix: **prayers are individual, audible, and answerable at ground level.**

- Zoom to a village and you *hear* petitions: heal my daughter; rain for the harvest; let my rival's boat sink; give me a sign.
- Answering (or pointedly not) is the micro-loop: it shapes Interpretation tags, mints currency, promotes Figures ("the girl the god healed" grows up a saint — or a tyrant), and writes chronicle entries.
- Prayers have systemic stakes, not vending-machine outcomes: heal the warlord's son and you've chosen a side in a succession war you haven't noticed yet.
- A prayer *inbox* (curated highlights) exists at higher zooms so the feature scales, but the full murmur is only down at ground level — making the ground view the emotionally intimate zoom, not just the pretty one.

## Entropy of belief (why you keep playing)

- **Mundanification:** miracles normalize. Two generations after the golden mountain, it's just "the mine." Wonders need Devotion upkeep to stay *miraculous*; lapsed wonders become mundane (or ruins, or heresies).
- Myths mutate (doc 02's fame/rumor propagation): your deeds are misremembered, credited to heroes, or claimed by rival faiths. If you care about your story, you have to keep tending it.
- Together these make faith a living economy with sinks, not a mounting score — and give the no-opponent sandbox its gentle, perpetual pressure.

## The world wears your reputation (dynamic tone)

Per-region, driven by the local faith mix — never a global evil-slider:

- **Dread regions:** ashen palettes, gothic verticality creeping into architecture, plague-doctor silhouettes, crowds that kneel *away* from your avatar, keening choral stems in the score.
- **Devotion regions:** golden-hour warmth, garden shrines, processions, children playing miracle-reenactment games.
- **High-Doubt regions:** gray civic austerity, defaced shrines, philosophy salons.
- Implementation is tractable: region-keyed palette/post grading, swappable architecture kit trims, crowd-behavior parameters, music stems. Diegetic > post-filter wherever possible.
- One world can hold all of these at once — which is better for storytelling, screenshots, and streams than a single global mood.

## Open questions (parked, not forgotten)

- Can players *write scripture*? (Letting the player author a commandment set that AI cultures then obey/distort is a wild feature — flag for post-core exploration.)
- Do dead Figures go somewhere? An afterlife/underworld layer is a natural expansion but pure scope creep for now.
- Pantheon NPCs in solo (multiple AI gods with personalities) — defer; the faceless rival is the right v1.
